## Introduction

Have you ever gone to bed, but your mind refused to rest?

Your body was tired, but your thoughts kept running. You thought about the bills. You thought about your family. You thought about your health, your work, or a decision you had to make. Then one question kept returning: “What if something goes wrong tomorrow?”

Worry often begins with two small words: “What if?”

What if I fail? What if there is not enough money? What if someone I love does not get better?

We try to solve tomorrow while living today. We carry problems that have not happened. We fight battles that may never come. Fear takes today’s strength and spends it on tomorrow’s questions.

Jesus understands this struggle. In Matthew 6, he spoke to ordinary people who worried about food, clothing, safety, and the future. Their world was different from ours, but their hearts were much the same. They wanted to know, “Will we have enough? Will we be safe? Who will take care of us?”

Jesus did not laugh at them. He did not shame them. He pointed them to their Father in heaven.

God does not give every answer about tomorrow, but he gives his presence and enough grace to trust him today.

We may not know what tomorrow will bring. But we can know the One who will meet us there.

## 1. Receive Today as the Day God Has Given You

> Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.
>
> *Matthew 6:34, World English Bible*

Jesus does not say that tomorrow has no trouble. He says that tomorrow’s trouble belongs to tomorrow.

That is important. Jesus is not asking us to pretend that life is easy. He knows that each day can carry pain, pressure, and hard work. But he also knows that we were not made to carry every day at the same time.

Think about a person carrying bags from the market. One bag may be manageable. Two may be heavy. But imagine trying to carry every bag you will need for the next year. You could not walk. The weight would crush you.

Worry does something like that. It piles next week, next month, and next year onto your shoulders. It tells you that you must understand everything now. It makes you feel responsible for things only God can control.

Jesus brings us back to today.

Today, you can pray. Today, you can tell the truth. Today, you can do the work in front of you. Today, you can forgive someone. Today, you can ask for help. Today, you can take one faithful step.

Faith is not refusing to plan. Planning says, “I will act wisely and trust God with the result.” Worry says, “Everything depends on me, and I must control what happens.”

Jesus invites you to make plans with open hands. Do today’s work, but leave tomorrow in the Father’s care.

God has given you this day. Do not let an imagined tomorrow steal it.

## 2. Give God the Weight You Cannot Carry

> Cast your burden on Yahweh, and he will sustain you. He will never allow the righteous to be moved.
>
> *Psalm 55:22, World English Bible*

A burden is anything that feels too heavy for your heart. It may be fear, grief, guilt, responsibility, or a question without an answer.

The verse says, “Cast your burden.” It does not say, “Hide your burden.” It does not say, “Pretend you are strong.” God invites you to bring the full weight to him.

Imagine a child carrying a large container of water. The child wants to help, but the container is too heavy. The child struggles and almost drops it. Then the father reaches down and says, “Give it to me.”

The wise child does not say, “No, I must prove that I can carry it alone.” The child lets go.

Many of us pray about our worries but never release them. We tell God about the burden, say “Amen,” and then pick it up again before we leave the room.

Surrender may need to happen many times. When the fear returns, pray again. When your mind begins to build a frightening future, return to God again. This is not failure. It is the daily practice of trust.

You can pray in simple words:

“Father, I cannot control this situation. I will do what is right, but I give the result to you. Please carry what I cannot carry.”

God may not change the situation at once. But he promises to sustain you. To sustain means to hold you up and give you what you need to keep going.

God may sustain us through Scripture, through people who help us, or through quiet strength that cannot be explained. The problem may still be real, but panic no longer has to rule the heart.

Do not confuse God’s care with getting every result you want. God’s care is deeper than that. He is present when the answer is yes, when the answer is no, and when the answer is “wait.” His love is not measured by how easy your week becomes.

How do we know he cares? We look at Jesus.

> He who didn’t spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how would he not also with him freely give us all things?
>
> *Romans 8:32, World English Bible*

Paul wrote these words to Christians living under pressure. He did not promise that they would avoid pain. He pointed them to the cross. God had already given his Son for them, so suffering could not prove that God had stopped loving them.

At the cross, Jesus carried our greatest burden—our sin. He took the judgment we deserved. Then he rose from the dead, defeating sin and death. If God has already given his Son to save us, we can trust his heart when life does not make sense.

You do not need to carry alone what Jesus has invited you to surrender.

## 3. Trust the Savior Who Is With You in the Storm

> He awoke and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” The wind ceased, and there was a great calm. He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?”
>
> *Mark 4:39-40, World English Bible*

Jesus and his disciples were crossing the Sea of Galilee when a violent storm arose. Water entered the boat. The disciples believed they were going to die. Jesus was with them, but they still faced a storm.

Following Jesus does not mean storms will never come. Faith gives us Someone to trust when our strength is gone.

The disciples looked at the wind and believed the storm had the final word. Then Jesus stood and spoke.

“Peace! Be still!”

The wind obeyed him. The sea became calm.

The greatest miracle was not only that Jesus stopped the storm. The miracle also showed who was in the boat. Jesus was not merely a teacher. He was the Lord over creation. The storm was powerful, but it was not more powerful than him.

You may be asking Jesus to calm a storm in your life. Keep asking. He is able to act.

But remember this: sometimes Jesus calms the storm around us, and sometimes he calms his child while the storm continues.

Either way, you are not alone in the boat.

Think of a small child walking through a dark room while holding a parent’s hand. The room does not become brighter at once. The child may still hear strange sounds. But the child can keep walking because someone trustworthy is near.

Peace is not always having a clear path. Peace is knowing who holds your hand.

Jesus entered our deepest storm. He faced rejection, suffering, the cross, and death. He did this willingly to bring us back to God. On the third day, he rose again. Because Jesus lives, no storm can remove us from his love. Even death does not have the final word.

This is why Christian peace is more than positive thinking. It is built on a risen Savior.

Fear says you are alone. The good news says Jesus is with you. Guilt says God will not receive you. The good news says everyone who trusts in Jesus is forgiven and welcomed.

Today, Jesus is not asking you to understand your whole future. He is asking you to trust him with your next step.

What is that step for you?

Perhaps it is praying honestly, asking someone for help, or obeying something God has already made clear.

Perhaps it is coming to Jesus for the first time and saying, “Lord, I cannot save myself. I believe you died and rose again. Please forgive me and lead my life.”

The storm may be loud. But Jesus is still Lord.

## Conclusion

Tomorrow may contain things you cannot predict. There may be answers you do not yet have.

But tomorrow will not arrive before God does.

The Father who cares for you today will still be God tomorrow. The Savior who died and rose for you will still be with you tomorrow. The Holy Spirit who strengthens believers will still give help tomorrow.

So come back to the grace God is giving now.

Receive today as his gift. Give him the weight you cannot carry. Trust the Savior who is with you in the storm.

God does not give us all the answers for tomorrow, but he gives us his presence and enough grace to trust him today.

You do not have to carry tomorrow tonight.

## Closing Prayer

Father, you know every person who feels tired, afraid, or unsure about the future. You see the parent worried about the family, the worker worried about a job, the person waiting for medical news, and the believer who feels weak in faith.

Please forgive us for trying to control what belongs to you. Teach us to do today’s work with faith and to leave tomorrow in your hands. Carry the burdens that are too heavy for us. Give wisdom where decisions must be made, courage where action is needed, and patience where we must wait.

Lord Jesus, thank you for entering our storm. Thank you for carrying our sin on the cross and rising again. Speak your peace into our hearts. Help us remember that we are never alone and that no storm is greater than you.

Holy Spirit, guide our next step. Give us enough grace for today. We place our future, our families, our needs, and our lives in your hands.

In the name of Jesus, amen.
