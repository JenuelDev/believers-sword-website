Water is entering the boat, and the person the disciples turn to is asleep. In Mark 4:35-41, that contrast brings their fear into the open. They wake Jesus and question whether he cares about their lives.

Mark has just described Jesus teaching by the sea. When evening comes, Jesus tells his disciples, the people learning from him, to cross to the other side. A great windstorm rises during the journey. Waves beat into the boat. Jesus is in the stern, the back of the boat, asleep on a cushion.

The account gives us a real danger within the story. It does not require us to guess every thought in the boat, or invent a reason why Mark included each detail, to follow what happens next.

## 1. They bring an accusation with their request

The disciples call Jesus Teacher and ask whether he cares that they are perishing. Perishing here means dying. Their words carry both a plea for help and a challenge to his care.

Fear can do that to our own speech. A delayed answer begins to feel like proof that we have been forgotten. Yet the feeling and the conclusion are different. The disciples know the boat is filling. Whether Jesus cares is the question they add to that fact.

There is room here to pray before you have arranged every feeling neatly. Hebrews 4:15-16 invites believers to approach Jesus for mercy and help because he understands human weakness. You can name the fear honestly while asking him to help you understand what is true.

That is different from treating every angry conclusion as correct. Honest prayer makes room for correction as well as relief.

## 2. Jesus acts before asking his questions

Jesus wakes, addresses the wind, and commands the sea to be quiet. The wind stops and there is a great calm. Only then does he question the disciples.

> He said to them, "Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?"
>
> *Mark 4:40, World English Bible*

The order matters. Jesus has already acted to save them from the storm when he asks about their trust. His question still deserves to be heard. We do not need to make it less serious in order to recognise his care.

The scene also recalls Psalm 107:23-30, where sailors cry to the Lord in danger and he stills a storm. Read the two passages together. Mark's account directs attention to the authority of the person in the boat, not merely to a method for feeling calm.

## 3. The calm leaves them with a larger question

The disciples become greatly afraid after the wind stops. They ask who Jesus is, since even the wind and sea obey him. Their fear has changed its focus. The immediate danger has passed, and they are confronted with someone whose authority exceeds what they understood.

This ending guards us against using the story as a simple formula: ask correctly and every difficulty will disappear immediately. Jesus speaks about trouble for his followers in John 16:33. The rescue Mark records reveals who Jesus is; it does not give us a timetable for every rescue we will ask for.

**Try this today:** Name one fear in a plain prayer. Then read the disciples' final question in Mark 4:41. What has the passage shown you about Jesus that your first frightened thought left out? If there is a practical step to protect yourself or help someone else, take it while you pray.

The disciples leave this scene with more to learn. You do not have to pretend that your questions are finished either. Bring the danger honestly, receive the correction seriously, and keep attending to the person Mark places at the centre of the story.
